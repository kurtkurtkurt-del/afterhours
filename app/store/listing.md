## full description (4000)

### en

afterhours is for deciding whether to go out tonight.

there is no search and no endless feed. the deck hands you one night at a time: a club night, a rave, a concert, a festival. swipe right to keep it, left and it never comes back, up for the ticket, down for the whole page.

see who is going. your friends' kept nights, who is coming (in, maybe, out), and a match when you both keep the same one. nothing you let go is ever shown to anyone.

start a night nobody has organised yet. a spark is a plan in one tap: grill by the river, a hike out of the city, karaoke, a night swim, a jazz basement. send it to your friends, or let it reach their friends too, and watch who is in.

go together. a group finds a night by swiping the same deck, votes, sets the plan and keeps the photos of the night in one album. message a friend directly, or nudge them about a night with the link.

listen before you go. many nights carry a 30 second preview of the act, and a few lines on who is playing.

check in at the door and the night becomes a card, yours to keep. the room after the night stays open for 48 hours, for the people who were there.

the map shows tonight's nights around you.

your people, your rules. a block closes both ways, people, comments, group chats and posts can be reported, and you can download your data or delete your account in settings at any time. afterhours is 18+.

background music if you want it, off by default. in english, deutsch and türkçe.

we count nights out, not minutes in.

## data safety (answers)
- location: approximate + precise, collected, not shared, optional (map only), used for app functionality
- email address: collected for account creation and sign-in; optional (guest mode exists)
- user-generated content: profile (handle, name, bio, photo, links), comments, room posts, direct messages, group chats and album photos, posts with likes and comments, sparks — **sabah Ahmet**: the old line said "comments in rooms (later)"; Data safety must now declare messages, photos and other UGC

- app activity: swipes (kept/let go) tied to the account; never shown per person, only totals
- no advertising id, no analytics sdk, no third-party sharing
- data is encrypted in transit (https); users can request deletion in-app (settings → delete account) or on the website
